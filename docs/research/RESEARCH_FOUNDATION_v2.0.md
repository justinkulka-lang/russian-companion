# RESEARCH FOUNDATION v2.0
## Premium Offline Russian Self-Teaching Companion

**Status:** Evidence dossier for product reset  
**Date:** 22 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Establish what a serious beginner Russian product must teach, how it should guide independent study, and which content can lawfully be distributed offline.

---

## 1. Executive conclusion

The failed v1.1.0/v1.1.1 product line proved that a schedule, thirty numbered containers, gates, logs, and a catalog of unrelated words do not constitute a curriculum. A premium self-teaching product must begin with Russian literacy, teach and practice a deliberately sequenced body of knowledge, explain how each activity supports learning, and connect every assigned item to a verified instructional purpose.

The reset product must therefore be built around a **curriculum spine**, not a tracking spine. For this product, the controlling spine is the published sequence of *The New Penguin Russian Course*. The app begins with Cyrillic because Penguin Lesson 1 begins with learning to read Russian; it then follows Lessons 2–30 in order. App-authored or openly licensed support may prepare for and reinforce the current Penguin lesson, but it may not introduce later-book topics early merely because another curriculum orders them differently.

The app may remain fully offline and contain no runtime AI. Those constraints do not prevent a strong course. They require the instructional content to be deliberately prepared, reviewed, licensed, packaged, and tested before release.

## 2. Research questions

1. What must an absolute beginner learn first in Russian?
2. What sequence supports first-year reading, listening, speaking, and writing?
3. What does effective independent study require beyond content presentation?
4. What can be scored without runtime AI or a tutor?
5. What content can be redistributed inside an offline Android app?
6. How can the app coordinate with Penguin without reproducing protected material?
7. What makes the interface useful rather than cluttered?

## 3. Evidence hierarchy

The following hierarchy governs product decisions:

1. Official proficiency frameworks and university curricula.
2. Peer-reviewed second-language acquisition research and systematic reviews.
3. Publisher and rights-holder statements.
4. Established open educational resources with explicit licenses.
5. Experienced-user reports only as usability signals, never as linguistic authority.

Search-engine snippets, file names, community uploads, and a claim that something is “free” do not establish accuracy or redistribution rights.

## 4. What an absolute beginner must encounter first

### 4.1 Cyrillic is a prerequisite, not Lesson 1 decoration

Russian uses a 33-letter Cyrillic alphabet. University first-year programs consistently begin with Cyrillic, phonetics, spelling, and reading before expecting independent grammar or text work. The Sechenov University beginner curriculum begins its A1 description with “Phonetics and Graphics: Alphabet, sounds, stress.” First-year syllabi from Urbino, Perugia, Milan, and other universities similarly place the alphabet and phonetic system at the entrance to the course.

The app must therefore treat Penguin Lessons 1–2 as its required literacy foundation rather than placing an unrelated pre-course in front of the book. A learner may test out of individual foundation practice only through verified recognition and reading checks; the course sequence and book directions remain visible.

### 4.2 The foundation sequence

The exact Russian examples and recordings require qualified review, but the instructional sequence should cover:

1. Letter recognition and names.
2. Printed uppercase and lowercase forms.
3. Common italic/cursive forms as a recognition reference, with writing practice optional.
4. Sound correspondences, beginning with low-confusion letter groups.
5. Letters visually confusable with Latin letters.
6. Iotated vowels and their effect on preceding consonants.
7. The soft sign and hard sign.
8. Hard and soft consonant contrast.
9. Word stress.
10. Unstressed vowel reduction.
11. Voicing/devoicing and common consonant changes at a beginner-appropriate level.
12. Syllable blending, word reading, and short-phrase reading.
13. Russian keyboard installation and basic typing.

This foundation must combine explanation, audio, recognition, retrieval, reading aloud, listening discrimination, and listen–record–compare. It must not merely display an alphabet chart.

### 4.3 Early communication before grammatical completeness

Official A1 and first-year curricula emphasize functional communication alongside systematic grammar. Early content should enable the learner to recognize and produce rehearsed language for identity, greetings, locations, possessions, numbers, time, routine actions, preferences, requests, and basic questions. Grammar should be introduced when it enables those functions rather than as disconnected tables.

The official Penguin Random House table of contents establishes the following lawful topic-level sequence. This schedule controls the app. It does not authorize copying the book's prose, examples, exercises, translations, or answer key.

| 2 | Russian handwriting and additional pronunciation |

| 4 | Verbs, personal pronouns, present tense, conjugation, word stress, and introduction to nouns/cases |

| 6 | Possession, going places, accusative case, and personal names |

| 15 | Requests, imperative, and consolidation of aspect use |
| 16 | Instrumental case, selected demonstratives, and surname declension |
| 17 | Time, date, age, ordinal numbers, months, and years |
| 18 | Comparative, superlative, and relative clauses |
| 19 | Conditional, obligation, and prefixes |
| 20 | Verbs of motion: going, running, and bringing |
| 21 | Further possession and purpose |

The app may break a unit into smaller sessions, add prerequisite clarification, and provide extra practice for the current topic. It shall not move aspect before Lesson 12, verbs of motion before Lesson 20, participles before Lesson 27, or any other later topic ahead of its controlling lesson. Cumulative review may revisit earlier topics at any time.

Completion of this sequence does not prove A1, A2, fluency, or any ILR rating. CEFR and ACTFL describe multidimensional real-world performance; app completion is not a valid proficiency examination.

## 6. Learning design findings

### 6.1 Retrieval and spacing

Repeated retrieval improves vocabulary retention, and spacing repeated encounters is generally more effective than massing them. The app should coordinate daily retrieval and hand vocabulary/form scheduling to AnkiDroid unless an app-native scheduler is separately authorized. It should not rely on rereading alone.

### 6.2 Meaningful input plus deliberate study

Meaning-focused exposure produces incidental vocabulary gains, but the research shows substantial variation and limited proportional learning from exposure alone. A beginner product should combine comprehensible input with deliberate vocabulary, form, and pronunciation practice.

### 6.3 Pronunciation and listening

Shadowing and repeated listening can improve bottom-up listening and may support pronunciation, especially when learners can attend to phonemes, stress, rhythm, and intonation. The app can safely provide model playback, slowed model playback, recording, A/B replay, phrase looping, and guided shadowing. It must not score pronunciation without a valid assessment system.

### 6.4 Immediate explanatory feedback

For closed-response exercises, “correct/incorrect” is insufficient. Each answer must expose a concise explanation tied to the taught rule and a direct return to the relevant reference. Feedback must be pre-authored and reviewed.

### 6.5 Interleaving and cumulative review

Lessons should include targeted practice followed by mixed cumulative review. Gates should sample prior foundations instead of checking only the newest topic.

### 6.6 Product guidance is part of instruction

An independent learner needs explicit directions: what to study, in what order, for how long, what “done” means, what to repeat after difficulty, and when to consult the book, AnkiDroid, reference material, or a tutor. A mere list of resources transfers curriculum design back to the learner and fails the product purpose.

## 7. Relationship to *The New Penguin Russian Course*

Penguin Random House describes the book as a thirty-lesson beginner course with revision exercises. Its public table of contents confirms a progression through basic grammar, gender, present tense, conjugation, stress, nouns and cases, aspect, motion, and advanced first-course topics.

The app may lawfully coordinate with the user's copy by storing:

- Lesson number
- Publicly stated lesson title or a neutral identifier after rights review
- User-entered page range
- User-entered exercise numbers
- Independently written learning objectives
- Independently created or openly licensed explanations and practice
- A direction to complete work in the user's own copy

The app must not reproduce Penguin passages, exercise prompts, answer keys, translations, tables, or distinctive explanatory prose. A mapping is pedagogically valid only after a reviewer compares the actual owned edition and signs off that the app's objectives and prerequisites correspond to that lesson.

## 8. Content-source assessment

### 8.1 Candidate: FSI / U.S. government Russian materials

Third-party archives describe Foreign Service Institute materials as U.S. government public-domain works. “An Active Introduction to Russian” provides extensive course material and audio. These materials are promising for reusable dialogues, drills, and recordings, but each acquired file still requires provenance confirmation, an original-publication check, audio-speaker/source documentation where available, quality inspection, and modernization review. Public-domain status must not be inferred solely from a mirror's general statement.

**Disposition:** Candidate source; not automatically approved.

### 8.2 Candidate: *Между нами*

The official site describes a complete first-year Russian program and explicitly recognizes the special alphabet/sound-system focus of early Russian instruction. Its stated license is CC BY-NC-ND 3.0, while some supplements use other licenses including BY-NC-SA and BY-NC-ND. “NoDerivatives” makes adaptation, excerpt transformation, and integration into an app legally sensitive; “NonCommercial” also constrains distribution.

**Disposition:** Excellent sequencing reference and possible unchanged noncommercial resource only after legal review; not an automatic source for transformed app content.

### 8.3 Candidate: COERLL Russian materials

The University of Texas Center for Open Educational Resources and Language Learning lists Russian materials with differing licenses. Individual assets must be evaluated separately because a collection page may include CC BY, CC BY-NC, and NoDerivatives components.

**Disposition:** Per-asset review required.

### 8.4 Candidate: OpenRussian

OpenRussian structured dictionary text has been treated in the existing project as CC BY-SA 4.0. It can support verified headwords, stress, morphology, and frequency metadata. It is not a curriculum, its audio is not automatically open, and database extraction invokes share-alike obligations.

**Disposition:** Reference data only; never a lesson sequence by frequency rank.

### 8.5 Candidate: Shtooka/SWAC

Shtooka recordings can support native-model playback only when the exact audio file has a compatible license, known provenance, speaker/source metadata where required, and a one-to-one verified text match.

**Disposition:** Audio component only; never curriculum authority.

### 8.6 Candidate: Tatoeba

Sentence text and audio have separate licensing considerations; audio licensing can vary by recording. A sentence cannot enter the product merely because the text is reusable.

**Disposition:** Per-item text and per-file audio verification required.

### 8.7 Web references and “free” courses

Freely accessible websites, scanned commercial books, community mirrors, YouTube content, and unlicensed PDFs cannot be bundled. Links are also inconsistent with a fully offline product.

**Disposition:** Research reference only unless explicit redistribution rights are documented.

## 9. Required human authority

A premium language course cannot eliminate linguistic judgment. It can limit and govern it.

The reset requires a **qualified Russian Curriculum Reviewer** for a bounded pre-release engagement. This person must be proficient in Russian and competent to review beginner Russian pedagogy. The reviewer must approve:

- Cyrillic and phonetics explanations
- Every Russian example and translation
- Every answer key and distractor
- Stress marking and audio/text matching
- Lesson prerequisites and sequence
- Penguin companion mappings
- Grammar-reference accuracy
- Cultural/usage notes that could mislead a beginner

This role is a one-time content-release authority with versioned sign-off, not a perpetual employee and not a substitute for live speaking correction. Content changes after approval invalidate only the affected content batch and require re-review.

## 10. Product implications

1. The current catalog-order word bindings must be deleted.
2. Penguin Lessons 1–2 must serve as the required Cyrillic, handwriting-recognition, and pronunciation foundation of the pathway.
3. Lessons must teach, demonstrate, practice, review, and direct—not merely list tasks.
4. Objective closed-response work may be scored from reviewed answer keys.
5. Speech and open writing remain unscored; they may be saved for self-comparison or tutor review.
6. The app requires a compact reference library and searchable glossary.
7. The daily experience must present one clear next action.
8. Progress must distinguish activity, curriculum completion, and demonstrated closed-response performance.
9. Every content asset must carry provenance, rights, review status, and instructional placement.
10. Release must be blocked when required content is missing, placeholder, randomly assigned, or unreviewed.

## 12. Research limitations

- The exact Penguin lesson-to-topic mapping must be verified against the user's owned edition; public tables of contents are not enough for a release mapping.
- No third-party source is approved merely by being listed here.
- The availability of an asset today does not establish durable redistribution rights.
- A qualified reviewer remains necessary before Russian instructional content can ship.
- Physical usability on the Galaxy S26 Ultra remains a separate release condition.
